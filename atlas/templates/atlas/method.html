{% trans "Built from the novel" %}

{% blocktranslate %}This is a reasoned reconstruction of fictional geography, not a canonical or surveyed map. It is inspired by Stephen King’s novel IT, and the novel’s own descriptions are its only literary source. Film and television geography has not been used.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate %}The town plan combines both main periods and the historical interludes. Dates in the place cards distinguish former sites and replacements; the map does not depict a single historical day. It covers surface geography only.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% trans "What the symbols mean" %}

{% blocktranslate %}A · Text-anchored relationship. The street, adjoining feature or named site is explicit. The plotted coordinate remains reconstructed.
B · Inferred position. Routes, proximity and relative directions constrain the placement.
C · Proposed location. A substantial gap remains; the position is a plausible option.
U · Unlocated / off map. The place is indexed without an invented coordinate.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% trans "The novel" %}

{% blocktranslate %}Stephen King, IT (1986). References in the place cards name chapters and interludes. No text of the novel is reproduced on this website.{% endblocktranslate %}
